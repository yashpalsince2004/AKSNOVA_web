Machine learning allows computational systems to learn patterns directly from empirical data without being explicitly programmed with static rules.

## Core paradigms in Machine Learning

1. **Supervised Learning:** Training with labeled inputs and target outputs (e.g. classification of email spam, price regression).
2. **Unsupervised Learning:** Discovering hidden structure and clusters in unlabeled data (e.g. customer market segmentation).
3. **Reinforcement Learning:** Training autonomous agents through reward and penalty feedback in simulated environments.

## The essential algorithm toolkit

- **Linear & Logistic Regression:** The benchmark models for continuous estimation and binary probability calculation.
- **Decision Trees & Random Forests:** Non-linear decision boundaries with high interpretability and robust resistance to overfitting.
- **Gradient Boosting (XGBoost / LightGBM):** The gold standard for competitive modeling on tabular enterprise datasets.

## Overfitting vs. Underfitting

A critical skill for any machine learning engineer is diagnosing model bias and variance:
- **High Bias (Underfitting):** The model is too simple to capture trends. Fix by engineering more features or selecting more expressive architectures.
- **High Variance (Overfitting):** The model memorizes training noise and fails on unseen validation samples. Fix through regularization, dropout, pruning, and collecting more training instances.
